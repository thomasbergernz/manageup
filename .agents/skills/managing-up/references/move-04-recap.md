# Move 04 — The Recap

## Goal / Time

Send one recap after a meeting that mattered. 10–15 minutes, same day. Or next morning if it was a late one.

Whoever writes the recap writes the version of the meeting everyone remembers. Don't start from a blank page. Three input options:

- Paste the transcript from an AI note-taker (Otter, Fireflies, Granola, Fathom, Teams or Zoom transcription).
- Talk it through with a microphone or Wispr Flow.
- Your memory, talked out.

## Track switch

Same recipient logic as Move 02 — the recap goes to everyone in the meeting **plus** 1–3 people who weren't there but should know.

- **Boss track:** the +1s are how other teams find out what the user is driving.
- **Client track:** the +1s are how the client's CEO sees the work between the calls, so at renewal nobody has to wonder what was done all year.

3–7 people max. Skip recaps for status meetings, recurring 1:1s, and anything where the to-dos don't involve other people.

## Inputs to collect

Ask in one grouped message:

- **Situation:** track-constrained. Boss track — ask A (user ran the meeting or presented) or B (user is the person from another team who owns what happens next). Client track — C (client working session the user owns the follow-through on). Never boss + C, never client + A.
- **Meeting context:** transcript, notes, or talked-through. Paste or dictate.
- **Shape:** which of the three recap shapes applies — decisions-made, big-picture discussion, or alignment. Pick one.

## Prompt

Run verbatim, with situation and shape substituted:

> I'm drafting a recap to send after a meeting. Help me shape this.
>
> Situation: [A: I ran the meeting or presented / B: I'm the person from another team who owns what happens next / C: it was a client working session and I own the follow-through].
>
> Meeting context (transcript, notes, or talked-through): [paste or dictate]
>
> Pick the right shape:
> - If decisions got made: lead with the decisions, then who owns what, then the dates.
> - If it was a big-picture discussion: lead with where we landed, then decisions, then the risks I flagged.
> - If the goal was getting everyone on the same page: lead with what we discussed, where we landed, what's next.
>
> Format: one-sentence summary at the top so a busy person reading on their phone gets it in 5 seconds. Then 3–5 bullets. Close with what's next and anything still open. Calm and neutral, not chest-beating, not corporate-speak. Push back if anything sounds vague.

## Insight as guardrail

If the user skips this move, somebody else writes the version of the meeting that gets remembered — and it won't be theirs. Deliver the recap, then name the recipient gap: if the user listed only people in the room, ask "Who else should see this?" and suggest 1–3 candidates from the track's recipient logic. Never withhold the draft over a missing recipient list.

Format checks:

1. One-sentence summary at the top (busy person on phone, 5 seconds).
2. 3–5 bullets.
3. Close with what's next + anything still open.
4. Recipients list totals 3–7 people once named.
